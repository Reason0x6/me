Factual independence / deterministic laws / explanation

How Many Facts Does Reality Actually Contain?

World Rank, factual bases, and the geometry of explanation

A fact stops being independent when it becomes an unavoidable consequence of other facts and the laws connecting them.
AuthorGavin Austin
PublicationWorking Theory
StatusDefinition and theorem paper
RightsAll rights reserved
00

Abstract

A world may be easy to describe and hard to determine—or difficult to describe while almost entirely forced.

This paper formalises World Rank: relative to a fixed finite primitive schema and deterministic law, the minimum number of actual coordinate values required to distinguish one lawful world from every other lawful world. The definition separates descriptive compression from factual determination. A thousand independent zero-valued bits have low Kolmogorov complexity but World Rank one thousand; a visually irregular solution of a rank-990 linear constraint system has World Rank ten.

The central representation theorem identifies World Rank exactly with the minimum hitting-set number of the world’s disagreement hypergraph. From it follow a solver formulation and NP-hardness. Further results establish universal bounds, monotonicity under stronger laws, additivity for independent products, a finite-class counting bound, equality with nullity for affine linear systems, and a corrected inevitability bound for deterministic spacetime histories. A Law–Fact Frontier then compares the complexity of admissible laws with their residual World Rank, and Explanatory Leverage measures frontier descent per bit of additional law.

Keywords factual basis · teaching dimension · constraint systems · Kolmogorov complexity · algorithmic statistics · observability · cellular automata · explanation
Priority and scope

The finite invariant is not claimed as a new combinatorial object. Under the coordinate encoding below, target-specific World Rank is a teaching/specification number [1]; its global counterpart is a relational key [2]. The proposed contribution is the law-relative interpretation, theorem synthesis, inevitability analysis, Law–Fact Frontier, and factual-geography programme. Any priority claim beyond that requires a deeper review.

01

Before counting

Primitive rules

World Rank is relative, not absolute. It is meaningless until the variables, their domains, the law, and the admissible observations have been fixed.

Let the primitive schema be a finite indexed family X=(X1,…,Xn), with each variable taking values in a finite non-empty domain Ai. The ambient assignment space is Ω=∏i=1nAi. A deterministic law ℒ is represented extensionally by a non-empty solution set Sℒ⊆Ω. A complete actual world is one element w∈Sℒ.

Calling Sℒ deterministic does not mean that it contains one world. It means that the law is a fixed constraint on complete assignments. Residual degrees of freedom are represented by multiple lawful worlds. Initial conditions, boundary conditions, and exogenous inputs belong either in the law or among the primitive variables; they cannot hover ambiguously between the two.

R1

Fix the schema

Splitting one variable into ten or adding a variable that encodes the whole world can change an unweighted rank. Every comparison names one schema.

R2

Separate primitive and derived

A derived checksum may be a target fact, but it is not eligible as a basis fact unless the research design declares it primitive.

R3

Use exact law semantics

The law denotes all complete assignments it permits—not merely the equations as printed or one chosen simulation trace.

R4

Identify, do not merely predict

A basis must leave exactly one complete lawful world. High probability, local accuracy, and asymptotic recovery are different notions.

R5

Count observation cost explicitly

Cardinality is the baseline. Weighted rank uses declared costs such as log₂|Aᵢ|; exact real values require a separate continuous theory.

R6

Do not smuggle in causality

Logical determination within a solution set does not by itself establish temporal production, intervention response, or causal direction.

Admissible observations

Sometimes only a subset Q⊆[n] may be specified: initial conditions but not future cells, sensors but not hidden state, or boundary variables but not interior variables. This paper therefore defines a query-relative rank Rℒ,Q. The original World Rank is the full-coordinate case Q=[n]. If two lawful worlds agree on every coordinate in Q, no admissible basis can distinguish them and the query-relative rank is infinite.

02

Definition

World Rank

For B⊆Q, write wB for the restriction of the actual world to those coordinates. Its lawful fibre is the set of worlds still possible after those values are specified:

Fibℒ(w;B) = {u∈Sℒ : uB=wB}
Eq. 1

A specification removes alternatives by intersecting the lawful world-set with a coordinate cylinder through w.

Definition 1 / World basis

A subset B⊆Q is a world basis for w under ℒ when Fibℒ(w;B)={w}. The query-relative World Rank is

Rℒ,Q(w) = min { |B| : B⊆Q and Fibℒ(w;B)={w} },

with value ∞ if no such B exists. Write Rℒ(w) when Q=[n].

World Rank counts coordinate values, not their shortest description. The statement “all one thousand cells are zero” is short, but it supplies one thousand coordinate facts if the law permits every binary assignment. Conversely, a complicated-looking string can require few basis facts when constraints force the remaining coordinates.

Weighted World Rank

When variables have unequal domains or acquisition costs, assign ci>0 and replace cardinality by total cost. A representation-neutral theory does not emerge automatically—the costs are part of the model—but the choice ci=log₂|Ai| makes a uniformly coded finite variable contribute its raw capacity in bits.

Rcℒ,Q(w) = minB basis Σi∈B ci
Eq. 2

The cardinal rank is recovered with ci=1. No value-dependent compression is performed inside this definition.

Closure

The facts forced by a partial specification form a world-relative closure:

clwℒ(B)={i : ∀u∈Fibℒ(w;B), ui=wi}
Eq. 3

This closure is extensive, monotone, and idempotent. A basis closes to every coordinate. It need not satisfy the matroid exchange axiom, so “rank” is an analogy to degrees of freedom—not a claim that every law induces a matroid.

03

Central result

The Representation Theorem

To determine the actual world, a basis must rule out every lawful alternative at least once.

For each alternative world u∈Sℒ\{w}, define its admissible disagreement set

ΔQ(w,u)={i∈Q : wi≠ui}.
Eq. 4

The disagreement hypergraph Hℒ,Q(w) has vertex set Q and one hyperedge ΔQ(w,u) for each alternative lawful world.

Theorem 1Hitting-set representation

Let Sℒ be finite and w∈Sℒ. A set B⊆Q is a world basis if and only if it intersects every edge of Hℒ,Q(w). Therefore

Rℒ,Q(w)=τ(Hℒ,Q(w)),

where τ is the transversal—or minimum hitting-set—number, with τ=∞ when the hypergraph contains an empty edge.

Proof

If B misses the edge generated by some u≠w, then uB=wB, so the fibre contains at least w and u; B is not a basis. Conversely, if B hits every disagreement edge, every alternative u differs from w on at least one specified coordinate. No alternative remains in the fibre, hence the fibre is {w}. ∎

Corollary 1Computational hardness

Computing World Rank from an explicit finite binary world-set is NP-hard.

Proof

Reduce Minimum Hitting Set [11]. Given a hypergraph H on [n], choose w=0ⁿ. For every edge E, add one lawful world uE whose 1-coordinates are exactly E. The disagreement hypergraph at w is precisely H, so its minimum hitting-set size equals World Rank. ∎

SAT and constraint solving

Hard does not mean unusable. If a Boolean law is represented by a formula Φℒ(x), a proposed basis B is valid exactly when the following formula is unsatisfiable:

Φℒ(x) ∧ ⋀i∈B(xi=wi) ∧ (x≠w).
Eq. 5

A satisfying assignment is an explicit counter-world that the proposed facts failed to exclude. Counterexample-guided hitting set, MaxSAT, or mixed-integer optimisation can search for a minimum basis.

04

Consequences

Bounds and structural laws

Theorem 2Universal bounds and monotonicity

For the full-coordinate rank of any finite schema:

  1. 0≤Rℒ(w)≤n.
  2. Rℒ(w)=0 if and only if Sℒ={w}.
  3. Rℒ(w)=n if and only if, for every coordinate i, some lawful world differs from w only at i.
  4. If Sℒ′⊆Sℒ and w∈Sℒ′, then Rℒ′(w)≤Rℒ(w).
Proof sketch

The full coordinate set always identifies w; the empty set identifies it exactly when no alternative exists. Rank n means each set omitting one coordinate fails, which is equivalent to a counter-world differing only there. Strengthening a law removes alternatives and therefore removes hyperedges, never making a previous basis invalid. ∎

Theorem 3Additivity for independent products

Let two laws live on disjoint schemas, with lawful sets S₁ and S₂. For the product law S=S₁×S₂ and world w=(w₁,w₂),

RS(w)=RS₁(w₁)+RS₂(w₂).
Proof

The union of minimum bases for both components identifies the product world. Conversely, an alternative can change only the first component while holding the second fixed, so the first-coordinate portion of any product basis must identify w₁; symmetrically it must contain a basis for w₂. ∎

Theorem 4Counting bound for locally low-rank worlds

Assume every variable has an alphabet of size q. Let S≤r={w∈Sℒ:Rℒ(w)≤r}. Then

|S≤r| ≤ Σk=0r C(n,k)qk.
Proof

Assign each low-rank world a canonical minimum basis B and signature wB. Two distinct worlds cannot receive the same pair: if they did, each would survive the other’s identifying specification. There are at most C(n,k)qᵏ basis-signature pairs of size k. Sum through r. ∎

Local rank versus a global key

A global world basis is one coordinate set whose projection is injective on all of Sℒ. Let its minimum size be G(ℒ). This is the candidate-key or unique-column-combination problem of relational theory [2]. It obeys

maxw∈Sℒ Rℒ(w) ≤ G(ℒ),    G(ℒ) ≥ ⌈logq|Sℒ|⌉.
Eq. 6

The first inequality can be strict because different worlds may have different short signatures even when no one short coordinate set identifies them all.

05

Exact reductions

Linear systems and spacetime

5.1 Independent binary cells

Let S={0,1}n. For every world and every coordinate i, flipping only i produces another lawful world. Theorem 2 gives R(w)=n. In particular, 0ⁿ has World Rank n even though its Kolmogorov complexity is O(log n).

Theorem 5Linear nullity theorem

Let A∈𝔽qm×n, let b be consistent, and let S={x∈𝔽qn:Ax=b}. For every w∈S,

RA,b(w)=n−rank(A).
Proof

The solution set is the affine space w+ker A, whose dimension is d=n−rank(A). A coordinate set B identifies w exactly when the coordinate projection PB is injective on ker A. An injective linear map from a d-dimensional space into 𝔽q|B| requires |B|≥d. Conversely, among the coordinate functionals restricted to ker A, choose d that form a basis of its dual; their projection is injective. Thus the minimum is d. ∎

This is the precise sense in which World Rank extends linear degrees of freedom. The equality depends on the solution set being affine. General nonlinear constraints do not satisfy exchange, do not have equally sized minimal bases, and require the hitting-set definition.

5.2 Deterministic spacetime

Let a finite system have N cells over alphabet A and deterministic global update F:AN→AN. A history of T transitions contains N(T+1) cell-values:

h(s)= (s,F(s),F²(s),…,FT(s)).
Eq. 7

The lawful history set contains one trajectory for every initial state s∈Aᴺ.

Theorem 6Deterministic-history bound

For every history h(s), with all spacetime cells eligible as basis facts,

R(h(s))≤N    and    I(h(s))≥T/(T+1),

where I=1−R/[N(T+1)]. If admissible bases are restricted to the N initial-cell coordinates, then the query-relative rank is exactly N and I=T/(T+1).

Proof

The complete initial slice determines the trajectory, so unrestricted rank is at most N. If only initial coordinates may be queried, omitting any initial cell permits a different initial state—and hence a distinct complete history because the initial slice itself belongs to the history. Therefore all N are necessary. Substitute into the definition of I. ∎

Why this is an inequality

A later cell can sometimes uniquely identify one exceptional initial state, even though it cannot identify every initial state. For example, a deterministic update may send 00 to 00 and every other two-bit state to a state beginning with 1. Observing the first future bit as 0 identifies the entire history of 00; its unrestricted rank is one, not two. “The initial state determines the future” proves an upper bound unless the query rules require initial facts.

5.3 Continuous systems

An exact real-valued fact contains unbounded discrete information, so finite cardinal World Rank should not be transferred literally. The local analogue is differential. If regular constraints g(x)=0 have Jacobian rank r at w∈ℝⁿ, the solution set is locally an (n−r)-dimensional manifold. A suitable subset of n−r ambient coordinates forms a local chart by the implicit-function theorem. This recovers local differential degrees of freedom, while global uniqueness may require more observations because disconnected solutions can share a chart value. Nonlinear observability rank conditions belong to this neighbouring theory [7].

06

Separation

Not Kolmogorov complexity

World A

0 0 0 0 0 0 … 0

K(w)=O(log n)Rℒ(w)=n

Every coordinate is free under the law. The world is easy to print but no coordinate value follows from the others.

World B

1 0 1 1 0 0 … 1

K(w)≈nRℒ(w)=10

A ten-dimensional affine solution can look algorithmically irregular while all remaining coordinates are forced.

Kolmogorov complexity asks for the shortest program that outputs an individual string. World Rank asks for the smallest coordinate evidence that singles the string out among alternatives admitted by a named law. One is description-relative; the other is law-and-schema-relative. Neither bounds the other in general.

Difference from residual set size

Algorithmic statistics studies the trade between model complexity and the logarithm of the size of a model containing the data [3][4]. World Rank replaces log|S| with the coordinate geometry around the actual world. The two residuals can diverge sharply.

Large set / tiny rank

Let S={0ⁿ}∪{x:x₁=1}. The set contains 1+2ⁿ⁻¹ worlds, but the fact x₁=0 uniquely identifies 0ⁿ. Thus R(0ⁿ)=1.

Small set / maximal rank

Let S={0ⁿ,e₁,…,eₙ}. Only n+1 worlds are lawful, yet each coordinate is needed to rule out its corresponding unit vector. Thus R(0ⁿ)=n.

The difference is structural. Set size counts how many alternatives remain. World Rank asks how those alternatives overlap the coordinates of one actual world. The same number of alternatives can be easy or difficult to eliminate with primitive facts.

07

Model selection

The Law–Fact Frontier

A law can make facts disappear by encoding them. The honest object is therefore not one rank, but the trade between the cost of the law and the factual freedom left behind.

Fix the primitive schema, a prefix universal machine U, and an admissible family ℋ of computably represented laws. Every ℒ∈ℋ must denote a non-empty finite set Sℒ containing the actual world w. Define the rank structure function

FU,ℋw(k)=min {Rℒ(w): ℒ∈ℋ, w∈Sℒ, KU(ℒ)≤k}.
Eq. 8

The Law–Fact Frontier is the lower Pareto boundary of achievable points (KU(ℒ),Rℒ(w)).

Theorem 7Frontier bounds

Where finite, Fw(k) is integer-valued, non-increasing, and lies in [0,n]. If ℋ contains the unconstrained law, the frontier reaches rank n at constant law complexity up to encoding overhead. If it contains singleton laws, it reaches rank 0 by complexity at most KU(w)+O(1).

Proof

Increasing k enlarges the feasible law set, so the minimum cannot increase. Universal bounds come from Theorem 2. The unconstrained law admits the full product and has rank n. A program containing a shortest description of w can define the singleton set {w}, whose rank is zero. ∎

Explanatory Leverage

For two frontier budgets k₂>k₁, define secant leverage as the number of rank units removed per additional bit of allowed law:

Λ(k₁,k₂)= [Fw(k₁)−Fw(k₂)] / (k₂−k₁).
Eq. 9

Because the frontier is non-increasing, 0≤Λ≤n/(k₂−k₁). Marginal leverage is λ(k)=F(k−1)−F(k), and rank reductions telescope across a sequence of budgets.

This formalises “facts explained per bit of theory” only after the law language and primitive schema are fixed. Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable and changes by an additive constant with the universal machine. Practical work must publish a computable code length ℓ(ℒ)—for example a grammar, circuit, equation language, or solver encoding—and treat leverage as representation-relative.

A July 2026 paper on Representational Complexity similarly shows that arbitrary structure-plus-rule formalisms can shift information between components unless admissible representations are restricted [5]. The Law–Fact Frontier adopts that restriction explicitly. Recent work on causal description gaps quantifies extra bits required between observational, interventional, and counterfactual answer oracles [6]; World Rank addresses a different residual: which actual coordinate values still distinguish a complete lawful world.

08

Factual geography

Where are the facts?

A minimum basis need not be unique. Any spatial map of “the independent facts” must represent that ambiguity instead of selecting one convenient basis and calling it reality.

Let ℬmin(w) be the set of all minimum world bases. Define the minimum-basis participation of coordinate i and the factual backbone:

pi(w)= |{B∈ℬmin(w):i∈B}| / |ℬmin(w)|,    C(w)=⋂B∈ℬminB.
Eq. 10

pᵢ measures participation under the uniform distribution over minimum bases; C contains facts required by every minimum explanation.

Proposition 1Conservation of basis participation
Σi=1n pi(w)=Rℒ(w).
Proof

Average the identity Σᵢ1[i∈B]=|B|=R over all minimum bases and interchange the two finite sums. ∎

Q1

Do minimum bases concentrate in initial conditions, boundaries, defects, or interfaces?

Q2

Can a small spatial region participate in every basis for an arbitrarily large distant region?

Q3

Can minimum bases converge to fractal sets as spacetime resolution increases?

Q4

Does chaos change factual rank, or only the sensitivity and cost of recovering a fixed basis?

Q5

Which phase transitions appear as discontinuities in rank, backbone, or basis multiplicity?

Q6

Can equal-entropy systems have radically different disagreement hypergraphs and World Rank?

These are questions, not consequences of the definition. In particular, emergence does not automatically “collapse rank,” and chaos does not create independent facts in a closed deterministic history. The framework becomes scientifically useful only when those claims are converted into families of laws, asymptotic limits, and measurable comparisons.

09

From concept to research

Programme and limits

01

Boolean constraints

Encode laws in CNF or circuits. Use SAT counter-worlds and incremental hitting set to compute exact bases, backbones, and near-minimum bases.

02

Linear and algebraic systems

Use nullity in the affine case; Gröbner bases, elimination, and finite-field solvers for polynomial constraints. Compare algebraic dimension with coordinate World Rank.

03

Cellular automata

Compute rank by rule, history length, boundary condition, and admissible time slices. Distinguish initial-slice rank from unrestricted diagnostic rank.

04

Graphs and networks

Treat completions consistent with graph laws as possible worlds. Search for edge or vertex facts that identify one completion among alternatives.

05

Structural causal models

Define separate observational, interventional, and counterfactual world-sets. Never infer causal basis claims from observational equality alone [12].

06

Continuous dynamics

Use local observability and differential rank for exact-state reconstruction, then study global branch ambiguity separately.

What must remain fixed

World Rank is not invariant under arbitrary re-encoding. A single new primitive variable whose value names the whole world collapses rank to one. Duplicating derived facts inflates the denominator of Inevitability. Grouping bits into one large-domain coordinate can reduce cardinal rank without reducing specification cost. These are not edge cases; they are the reason a published result must include the schema, domains, eligible basis coordinates, target facts, law semantics, and cost model.

What the framework does not measure

World Rank is not entropy, mutual information, logical depth, computational irreducibility, causal influence, stochastic complexity, or the runtime needed to derive consequences. Two worlds can have the same rank while one requires astronomical computation from its basis. A future theory can place derivation cost beside World Rank, but should not hide both inside one number.

Defensible publication claim

World Rank is a domain interpretation and synthesis of an existing specification invariant, not a newly discovered invariant. The paper contributes explicit schema rules, the disagreement-hypergraph theorem in world language, structural results, the corrected spacetime bound, a rank-based Law–Fact Frontier, explanatory leverage, and minimum-basis factual maps.

10 / Conclusion

An explanation is a mechanism that turns facts into consequences.

World Rank asks how many actual values must survive that conversion. It does not reward a world for being easy to print, and it does not reward a law merely for being short. It asks which alternatives the law still permits and how much primitive evidence is required to eliminate them around this world.

The Law–Fact Frontier then asks the more scientific question: how many facts does a law buy? A powerful theory moves down the frontier efficiently. But the movement is meaningful only inside a declared language and schema. The discipline is the point. Without it, facts can be hidden inside laws, laws can be hidden inside variables, and inevitability can be manufactured by duplicating consequences.

With those rules fixed, “How many facts does reality contain?” becomes a family of exact finite problems, solvable special cases, hard optimisation problems, and careful continuous analogues. The answer is not a universal number. It is a geometry of determination.
